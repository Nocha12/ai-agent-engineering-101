# Rank cohort and payment integrity (schema 2)

The selector records `rank_selection`: version, actual warranty tier, candidate shop names and whether that tier has no fitting ask. Learning uses this recorded eligibility and the kept shop's talk band. A short-tier fit does not remove a normal-tier no-fit observation. Rows without reconstructible tier evidence increment `nofit_population_unknown`; they do not enter no-fit learning. Selection and all existing limits, warranty tiers and talk guards stay in their existing order.

No-fit is off for legacy `nofit: true` and unversioned `nofit: "ab"` switches. The corrected experiment requires all of these fields in the existing enabled rank switch:

```json
{"enabled": true, "nofit": "ab", "nofit_version": 2,
 "nofit_experiment": "new-reviewed-experiment-id", "nofit_epoch": 1794140000}
```

This is a schema example, not an instruction to change a running controller. The epoch must be positive and no later than assignment time. Use a fresh reviewed experiment ID and epoch; never combine old and corrected cohorts. This change does not write the switch.

`state()` assigns by opaque order ID and appends an assignment to `buyer_rank_pg_assignments.jsonl` before reading the rank cache. It records experiment, version, epoch, invocation, arm, assignment status, treatment status and reason. Journal records use an exclusive file lock, append mode, partial-write completion and fsync. A cold cache, a cache lock failure or a cache exception preserves assignment and falls back with an explicit untreated reason. Invalid IDs preserve invocation records with unknown assignment. Abrupt termination after assignment and before selection leaves treatment unknown. No shops, no quotes, ineligible orders, control orders and absent scores have separate untreated reasons. Applying the B scoring policy counts as treatment even if its chosen shop equals the baseline; ordinary fit ranking does not count as no-fit treatment. Failure to persist assignment disables B treatment for that invocation. Only small metadata is journaled; statistics and local paths are omitted from measured metadata.

Before each journal append, the writer checks the last byte under the same exclusive lock. An unterminated tail from a partial write, error or abrupt exit is separated with a newline before the next record; all existing bytes remain unchanged. The separator and record share the partial-write loop and fsync, so another failed or interrupted append is handled by the next writer in the same way. Separator/read/write failure cannot report a successful assignment or enable B treatment. Malformed retained evidence increments `inputs.journal.malformed_lines` and prevents `all_observed_rows_linked`; it cannot identify the original lost assignment or its arm/treatment. This counter counts malformed lines, not lost invocations, and must not be added as confirmed or assigned ITT records. The later successfully persisted assignment remains observable even if its cache step raises `BaseException` before any cache event or terminal order. `coverage_certified` remains false.

The cache schema is also 2. A schema-1 cache cannot supply old no-fit populations or not-known-unpaid success counts to this implementation. Ranking observation is bounded to 30 MB of orders, 4 MB of payments and 30 MB of ledger, refreshed at the existing 30 minute interval. With `"ledger_cover": true` in the switch the ledger read instead reaches back to the oldest paid seq of the order window (doubling from 30 MB, at most 400 MB, probing only the first row's seq at each size and then streaming once): on 11/09 the 30 MB tail left 605 window payments unknown, the covering read 60 MB and 2, as a full read. All arms read the same statistics, so switching it is a time boundary common to every running study: switch it at a recorded time and write that time into the studies' records. A cache whose `observation.ledger_cover` differs from the switch is computed again at once, and assignment rows carry `ledger_cover: true` while it is on. Input diagnostics record missing/truncated files, malformed lines, the first selected row's hash/seq/time and maximum observed seq. A discarded first partial line or a tail miss does not prove absence. These diagnostics describe collection; they do not certify a complete ledger prefix or economic window.

Payments follow the local shared `ax-paid-deals.py` contract: use the last payment row for an exact order ID; `paid is False` means refused; confirmation requires `paid is True`, payment `ledger` matching settlement `seq`, and settlement `k=settle`, order, buyer and amount matching the payment's price. Numeric-string payment seqs are normalized. IDs are opaque, including URL-shaped values. Missing, malformed or mismatched evidence is unknown. Bare `paid=True` does not establish confirmation. The default buyer is this submission's `nocha12`; offline fixture callers may explicitly pass their buyer to `compute`/`payment_outcomes`.

Learning cells preserve `n`, `confirmed`/`k`, `refused`, `unknown`, `failed` and `resolved_n`. Unknown delivered outcomes are excluded from the probability denominator; an entirely unknown pool has `p=None`, not zero. Price ratios use only confirmed outcomes. Explicit non-delivered terminal attempts such as NO_VALUE remain opportunity failures. No economic value or zero return is imputed for unknowns. Resolving only some outcomes can still bias learned probabilities; these adaptive statistics are not an economic certification.

`rank_paid_fixture.json` retains real field types, prices, timestamps and the non-one seqs from the shared calculator's documented 2026-11-08 production examples. Order IDs, item/unit IDs and buyer were anonymized. It includes a refusal with no settlement and a paid order with an exact settlement. Tests separately mutate missing payment, last-payment reversal, malformed paid, seq, order, buyer and amount. This selected fixture is not a full production ledger, and no live market API or credentials are needed.

`reconcile_itt(market_dir, experiment, epoch, end)` is read-only. It streams full order and assignment-journal files, without the learning tail limits, and returns the union of observed epoch orders, including food, no-stock, invalid IDs, no-stats and journal-only early exits. Exact IDs deduplicate terminal rows and journal events; invocation IDs link invalid-ID records where available. It preserves unknown assignments/treatments instead of inferring them from successful ranking. An invalid-ID measurement without an invocation cannot be safely joined to a journal entry: `unlinked_order_rows` exposes that ambiguity and the observed total can include both. Missing or malformed inputs are explicit. `coverage_certified` remains false: completeness of upstream collection must be checked independently before claiming a full epoch denominator. This routine supplies no economic success estimate.

A and B share adaptive fit/no-fit statistics. A is not a fixed pre-experiment holdout, and mixed-policy A/B results do not establish the total effect of full ON. Concurrent deadline policies alter observed quotes and eligibility; filtering to A alone does not isolate the deadline effect. No deployment or economic-effect claim follows from offline tests.

Offline regressions use the fixed test interpreter and `unittest` on `test_rank_integrity`, `test_rank_nofit`, `test_buyer_rank_pg`, `test_sw_route`, `test_hard_limits` and `test_meta.EvidenceTest`. The task's external safe runner copies source to a temporary repository layout, injects temporary HOME/state/market/controller roots, removes inherited credentials, places recording/failing model/API sentinels first on PATH and blocks external Python socket connections. It snapshots protected local market/memory metadata and hashes before/after. A sentinel attempt or changed protected file fails the isolation result. Full discovery must be run through the same runner on the final clean commit by the parent; sandbox denial of nested execution or loopback binding is reported as unverified rather than a product regression.
